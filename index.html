01 · Business problem

Why this product?

People who own multiple wearables may encounter fragmented records, inconsistent metric definitions, incomplete synchronization, and health scores that are difficult to compare. The project investigates whether a companion platform can make compatible information more coherent and transparent.

PROPOSED SOLUTION

One permissioned view

Connect selected device ecosystems through supported platform interfaces, preserve source and unit information, and show where readings are missing or not comparable.

TARGET USER

Everyday health-conscious people

People who use one or more compatible smartwatches, rings or health apps and want understandable personal trends—not only athletes or fitness professionals.

Research hypothesis

Potential opportunity: source-transparent health intelligence.

Rather than claiming that aggregation is new, investigate whether a product can provide clearer source attribution, cross-provider metric comparability, missing-data explanations and accessible personal trends.

03 · Product strategy and requirements

What should the first release do?

The proposed MVP focuses on useful, explainable health information rather than trying to support every device and every metric at launch.

MVP capabilities

  • Account and supported-client access.
  • Connect through HealthKit on iOS or Health Connect on Android where compatible.
  • Display the categories the user authorized.
  • Preserve source, timestamp and original unit.
  • Show connection status, sync time and data gaps.
  • Present personal trends for supported metrics.
  • Allow disconnect, permission revocation and data deletion requests.

Not in the first release

  • Medical diagnosis or treatment guidance.
  • Guaranteeing access to every wearable metric.
  • Replacing manufacturer apps or firmware.
  • Building wearable hardware.
  • Unreviewed automated health advice.
  • Unapproved direct integrations with every vendor.

Example user story

As a wearable user, I want to connect a supported device or health platform so that I can view the health measurements I authorize in one place.

Acceptance: the app explains requested categories, requests permission, does not imply data was retrieved if access is denied, preserves source and units, and marks unsupported metrics as unavailable rather than zero.

05 · Data model and analytics

Use real, public wearable data for the technical demonstration.

The selected public dataset is the Zenodo crowd-sourced Fitbit dataset. The original record states that 30 eligible Fitbit users consented to submit personal tracker data, including minute-level physical activity, heart-rate and sleep monitoring, collected from March to May 2016.

30

Participants

Small historical sample. It is not representative of global wearable users.

2016

Collection period

Useful for demonstrating data handling—not current market behavior or cross-brand compatibility.

Proposed SQL model

13 relational tables are designed for providers, device models, connected user devices, consent, metric definitions, normalized observations, activity, sleep, synchronization and explainable insights. The schema seeds provider and metric reference metadata only; it does not contain real personal health records.

Closing summary

The goal is not another activity tracker.

Unified Health is a proposed companion that aims to make compatible wearable information easier to connect, trace, understand and use. The business analysis work tests the market opportunity, defines an MVP, documents requirements, models the data and plans how success could be measured.
